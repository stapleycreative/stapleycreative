## Part 1: The portfolio-wide problem

Craig has 20+ years of work spanning product design, illustration, entrepreneurship, 3D animation, brand systems, emotional design, and design leadership. The sheer volume creates a selection problem: what goes on the portfolio site and why?

The filter: **each case study must prove something the others don't.** If two projects prove the same thing, cut the weaker one. A recruiter reading the full portfolio should come away with a different proof point from each piece, and together they should paint a complete picture of what Craig brings.

### What recruiters at Series B-D companies actually care about

Craig's own experience getting hired at GiveCampus was instructive. The thing that mattered most wasn't pixel-level craft or a clever design system diagram. It was evidence of growing a company from small to $100M+. Business impact. Scale of responsibility. Design decisions that connected to revenue.

That said, different companies weight different things. A startup wants scrappiness and shipping speed. A mature product company wants systems thinking and team leadership. A mission-driven org wants evidence you care about more than metrics. The portfolio needs to cover multiple hiring angles without becoming a buffet.

## Part 3: iFit case study rewrite (detailed plan)

### The problem with the current version

The existing ifit.mdx tells one story: the design system. Token architecture, shared atoms, documentation rules, distributed ownership. It's a good story. But it's one thread from an 11-year tenure, and it undersells the actual scope of what happened.

The current case study mentions nothing about the checkout, the IPO, the team growth trajectory, or the user/revenue numbers that prove the scale was real. It reads like a design systems blog post, not a career-defining body of work.

### Proposed structure: Three acts

#### Act 1: The foundation (Design system as infrastructure) ~800 words

Keep the strongest parts of the current case study. The button audit (23 variants, 9 blues) is a great diagnostic open. The token-first vs. component-first decision is a real architectural choice with a defensible tradeoff. The three rules show systems thinking.

But compress it. The current version is ~2,500 words on this alone. It should be ~800. The design system is the foundation layer, not the whole building.

**What to keep:**
- The button audit as the inciting incident
- Token-first architecture decision and why
- The three rules (compressed to one paragraph each)
- Onboarding acceleration metric (3 weeks to 3 days)

**What to cut:**
- The detailed "How it changed the org" section (fold best lines into a metrics block)
- The lengthy explanation of each rule
- "What this proves" section (too on-the-nose, let the work speak)
- "What I got wrong" section (save the honesty for a different beat)

#### Act 2: The revenue proof (NordicTrack checkout) ~600 words

This is new. Doesn't exist in the current case study. The single strongest business impact story Craig has.

The NordicTrack checkout redesign was the DTC purchase flow on nordictrack.com. In fiscal year 2021, 44% of iFit's interactive hardware revenue flowed through DTC channels. Total company revenue hit $1.7B that year. A/B testing of the old site versus the new showed (per the Contentstack case study) immediate improvements in revenue, conversions, and site performance.

**What to include:**
- Business context (iFit's revenue trajectory: $700M to $1.7B)
- What the checkout redesign actually involved
- A/B testing methodology and results
- 44% DTC revenue figure as proof of the flow's significance
- Figma screens from the checkout file

**What to source:**
- S-1 filing (revenue data, DTC percentages)
- Contentstack case study (A/B testing confirmation)
- Craig's Figma checkout file (actual screens)

#### Act 3: The scope nobody expects (IPO + team + empathy programs) ~600 words

Also new. Craig created all documentation for iFit's public offering. Investor decks, the official public offering deck. That's not product design. That's operating at the executive level.

Combined with building the creative team from 1 to 20, and the empathy programs he instituted (onsite gym time, call shadowing, internal fitness competitions, real data in mockups, in situ testing), this proves Craig operated across the full stack of design leadership.

**What to include:**
- IPO documentation scope (what he created, not the content)
- Team growth: 1 to 20, covering product design, marketing, social, animation, copywriting
- The empathy programs (from the Wistia interview deck)
- Photo of Craig actually running on a NordicTrack while testing (from the deck)

**What to be careful about:**
- IPO details may have confidentiality constraints. Scope of work, not content of decks.
- Team growth must avoid generic "I managed people" framing. Focus on structure decisions.

#### Supporting artifacts (embedded, not separate sections)

- **iFit Profile hub:** Best-documented project. Dashboard, data viz, avatar customization, workout history. Link or embed screens. Framing from interview deck: "Users lacked a sense of progress."
- **Marriott prototype:** Thumbnail/teaser only. "$15K interactive Framer prototype for a touchscreen hotel pitch." NDA limits detail.
- **Wearables 3D work:** One-line mention for range.

### The headline

Current: "Scaling a design org from 20K to 6M users" (leads with org, not impact)

Options:
- "Eleven years. $1.7B in revenue. One design team."
- "From treadmill screens to a $1.7B platform"
- Something sharper. The title needs to be a hook, not a capability list.

### Proposed OutcomeBlock

```
problem: "A hardware company with 5 product surfaces, no shared design infrastructure, and a checkout flow that hadn't been touched in years."
change: "Token-first design system across all surfaces. Rebuilt DTC checkout. Created all IPO investor documentation. Grew the creative team from 1 to 20."
result: "Company grew from 20K users to 6M. Revenue hit $1.7B. Checkout A/B tests showed immediate conversion improvements. New designers productive in 3 days instead of 3 weeks."
```
